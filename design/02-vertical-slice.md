# INFERNO — Vertical-Slice Spec: Prologue & Circle 1

Screen-by-screen breakdown of the first two levels. Conventions:

- **Screens** are fixed-camera stages (occasional scripted pans), lettered
  P1–P9 (Prologue) and L1–L8 (Limbo).
- **☠ Deaths** list every way to die on the screen and the rule each death
  teaches. Every death respawns at the screen's checkpoint in under one
  second.
- **Virgil beats** are the silent companion interactions; the first three are
  marked **(V1) (V2) (V3)** as required by the brief.
- Cantos staged: Prologue = Cantos I–III; Limbo = Canto IV & V (Minos).

---

## PROLOGUE — "Dark Wood & Gate" (Cantos I–III)

Target length: 12–15 min. Palette: near-black greens and sodium-lamp amber.
No title plaque — the game opens cold.

### P1 — The Park at Night
Wide shot: an overgrown park path, city skyline glowing behind. Dante walks
in from the left, coat collar up. Fireflies, distant sirens.
- **Teaches:** walk, run (holding run makes the camera lead him — players
  learn the camera trusts motion).
- **☠ Deaths:** none. A safe screen to learn the stick.
- **Checkpoint:** screen entry.

### P2 — The Leopard
The path narrows between hedges. A leopard drops onto the path ahead —
sleek, wrong, too big — and paces. Approaching it triggers a lunge.
- **Teaches:** threats are read by animation. The leopard's shoulder-drop
  telegraphs the lunge; backing away resets it. The only way forward is a
  side gate ajar on the fence — **crouch** to slip under.
- **☠ Deaths:** (1) Walking into the leopard → lunge kill. *Lesson: watch
  the telegraph.* (2) Standing in the open too long → lunge. *Lesson: Hell
  does not wait for you.*
- **Checkpoint:** screen entry.

### P3 — The Lion (chase)
A service road along the park fence. The lion erupts from the treeline
behind — full-screen chase left-to-right. A collapsed fence section mid-way
must be cleared with a **running jump**; a low bough after it needs a
**crouch-slide**.
- **Teaches:** running jump, crouch at speed, and the game's chase grammar
  (camera pushes forward; hesitation kills).
- **☠ Deaths:** (1) Missed jump → caught. (2) Standing jump attempted →
  too short → caught. *Lesson: speed feeds the jump.* (3) No crouch at the
  bough → knocked down → caught.
- **Checkpoint:** chase start.

### P4 — The She-Wolf (unwinnable)
The road ends at the shuttered surface station. The she-wolf steps out of
the dark ahead — gaunt, patient — and advances one pace for every pace
Dante takes. There is no way past her. The only open route is down: a
broken grate at the station's base.
- **Teaches:** some screens are not fights or puzzles; they are doors
  closing. The player is *herded* underground and should feel it.
- **☠ Deaths:** (1) Approaching the wolf → kill. (2) Standing still for
  ~8s → she closes the gap → kill. *Lesson: down is the only direction
  left.*
- **Checkpoint:** screen entry.

### P5 — The Dark Ticket Hall — **(V1) Virgil's introduction**
Pitch dark except for Dante's silhouette. A point of lamplight approaches
from deep in the hall and resolves into **Virgil**: gray uniform from
another era, brass buttons, punch-clip on his belt. He raises the lamp,
looks Dante over, and beckons — then walks. His lamp is the only light.
- **Teaches:** **Follow/Signal**. Straying from the lamp radius plunges the
  screen to black for a beat before Virgil returns for you; pressing Signal
  makes Dante raise a hand and Virgil pause. No death here — the mechanic
  is taught with darkness, not blood.
- **☠ Deaths:** none. The introduction screen is sanctuary.
- **Checkpoint:** screen entry.

### P6 — The Gate — **(V2) the ticket**
A rusted service entrance, chained. Above it a broken departures board
cycles garbage characters; for half a second the full inscription flickers
legibly — *ABANDON ALL HOPE* — then is gone (unreadable on a second look;
players who screenshot it are rewarded). Virgil produces a ticket, feeds it
to a dead fare gate, and the chains release. The shutter opens halfway and
stops. Virgil crouches through; Dante must **crouch-crawl** after him.
- **Teaches:** Virgil opens what Dante cannot; Dante fits where the fixed
  world allows. Their division of labor for the whole game, stated in one
  door.
- **☠ Deaths:** none.
- **Checkpoint:** screen entry.

### P7 — The Vestibule (the futile)
A long mezzanine. A whirling advertising banner — blank, glossy — is blown
in an endless circle by grinding ceiling fans, and a queue of gray souls
chases it forever, faces down. Sparks rain from the fans where the crowd
runs thickest.
- **Puzzle:** the crowd is a moving wall. Cross the mezzanine by walking in
  the gaps in the circling queue; **crawl** under a low information desk to
  bypass the tightest bend. Joining the flow (walking with the crowd for
  more than a few steps) is fatal.
- **☠ Deaths:** (1) Caught in the crowd's path → trampled. *Lesson: read
  crowd flow like a conveyor.* (2) Lingering under the sparking fans →
  ignition. *Lesson: the ceiling is a hazard layer too.*
- **Checkpoint:** mezzanine entry and midpoint desk.

### P8 — The Acheron Crossing — **(V3) Charon refused, ticket shown**
Set-piece. A flooded turnstile hall, water black and waist-deep, turnstile
tops breaking the surface like pilings. **Charon** — the graveyard-shift
conductor, cadaverous under a peaked cap, eyes like lit coals — poles a
maintenance flat-car through the flood, ferrying a load of gray souls.
He sees Dante, stops, and *refuses*: a slow, unmistakable head-shake and a
pointing pole (the living do not ride). Virgil steps between, shows the
ticket that cannot be refused; Charon turns away in disgust and lets them
board. **Playable crossing:** the flat-car pushes through the hall; Dante
must **balance** (stick to counter the car's lurch at each submerged
turnstile), **duck** under hanging dead cables, and **step over** the
drowned souls who grab at the deck edge.
- **☠ Deaths:** (1) Missed duck → cable sweep into the water. (2) Ignoring
  the lurch → thrown overboard; the water itself is death (the drowned pull
  you under instantly). *Lesson: on water screens, the deck is the world.*
  (3) Standing on the deck edge → grabbed by the damned.
- **Checkpoint:** boarding, and midway across.

### P9 — The Far Shore (level out)
The flat-car grounds. As in the poem, the crossing ends the way Canto III
ends: a red flash deep in the tunnel, a rising quake-rumble that shakes
dust from the ceiling — and Dante faints. Smash cut to black. He wakes on
a bench in a vast, warm light: Limbo. (The faint is the level transition —
the poem's own scene-cut, used as ours.)
- **☠ Deaths:** none. Scripted.
- **Checkpoint:** level complete.

## CIRCLE 1 — "LIMBO" (Canto IV, ending on Canto V's Minos)

Title card: the gothic ribbon plaque — **LIMBO** — over a wide establishing
pan of the stratum. Target length: 15–20 min. Palette: the reference image —
brick-red platform, teal trains, cream signage. The mood is not danger but
*waiting*: Limbo's hazards are few and gentle until the very end, and the
level's difficulty is spatial, not lethal. Grief, not fire.

### L1 — The Bench
Dante wakes on a platform bench. Wide shot: an endless terminus, crowds of
ordinary people standing in perfect queues for trains that idle with doors
shut and never depart. Virgil waits, lamp doused — here there is light, a
sourceless warm glow. He gestures down the platform: *we walk.*
- **Teaches:** re-orientation; Follow works in crowds (Virgil's cap bobs
  above the crowd line as a landmark).
- **☠ Deaths:** none.
- **Checkpoint:** screen entry.

### L2 — The Surge
A stretch of platform packed shoulder-to-shoulder. When a ghost train
glides in (silent, lights on, empty), the entire crowd surges one step
toward it — opening a gap along the back wall for exactly as long as the
doors stay shut. When the train ghosts out, the crowd relaxes back and the
gap closes.
- **Puzzle:** move during surges, wait during lulls. An overturned luggage
  trolley midway must be **climbed** — costing one surge — so the crossing
  takes two cycles, planned.
- **☠ Deaths:** (1) Caught in the crowd when it relaxes → compressed
  against the wall. *Lesson: the crowd is a tide; move on its beat.*
- **Checkpoint:** entry and trolley.

### L3 — The Nursery
A quiet side-hall: rows of prams and small strollers, gently rocking, a
mobile turning over each — the unbaptized infants, staged with total
restraint. No hazard, no puzzle: a walk-through screen. The crowd noise
fades to a hush; even Virgil removes his cap.
- **Purpose:** pacing valley and tone anchor. Limbo's grief made specific.
  (Players consistently report this as the screen they remember.)
- **☠ Deaths:** none — deliberately. Hell's first stratum wounds without
  touching you.
- **Checkpoint:** n/a (safe).

### L4 — The Luggage Mountain
The platform is dammed by a mountain of abandoned suitcases rising to the
ceiling girders. **Climb** it: grab-points on trunk handles and strap
loops. The pile is live — stepping on marked unstable cases (they visibly
teeter) triggers a slide.
- **☠ Deaths:** (1) Unstable case → avalanche carries Dante off the far
  side. *Lesson: the environment telegraphs weight-bearing surfaces.* (2)
  Missed grab at the girder traverse → fall.
- **Checkpoint:** base and summit girder.

### L5 — The First-Class Lounge (the Noble Castle)
Canto IV's castle of the virtuous pagans, staged as a glass-walled
first-class lounge raised above the platform, reached through **seven brass
turnstiles** in sequence (the poem's seven gates). Inside, lit green and
gold: the great pagans at rest — philosophers on benches mid-gesture, poets
standing in circles, a laureled figure who nods at Virgil *as an equal*
(the poem's honored welcome — Virgil belongs here, and the player sees it).
- **Puzzle:** each turnstile admits Virgil (he has standing) but locks on
  Dante. At each gate, **Signal** Virgil at the correct moment — he holds
  the arm from the far side against the gate's re-lock timing, a
  seven-beat cooperative rhythm that grows one beat tighter per gate.
- **☠ Deaths:** none — failure just re-locks the gate. This is the
  no-stakes rehearsal of the Virgil-timing mechanic that *will* have stakes
  at Minos.
- **Checkpoint:** each pair of gates.

### L6 — The Platform Edge
Beyond the lounge the platform thins to a maintenance lip along the track.
The **third rail** glints below; ghost trains pass at intervals with a
two-light warning in the tunnel dark.
- **Puzzle:** traverse the lip — **walk** segments, two **standing jumps**
  over gaps — timed between trains. Recesses in the wall fit a **crouched**
  Dante when a train passes.
- **☠ Deaths:** (1) Touching the third rail → instant. *Lesson: some
  surfaces are absolutes.* (2) Caught outside a recess by a train. *Lesson:
  the two-light telegraph and the recess rhythm.*
- **Checkpoint:** each recess.

### L7 — MINOS (set-piece)
The terminus ends at a judgment hall. **Minos** fills it: a monstrous
ticket inspector, three meters of uniform gone wrong, and his tail *is the
queue barrier* — a segmented, belt-and-stanchion tail that uncoils across
the entire hall floor. Souls approach his lectern one by one; he coils the
tail around each — two loops, five loops, nine — and the floor beneath
that soul opens, dropping them to their line. The tail's coiling is the
hazard: it sweeps and re-lays across the hall in readable patterns keyed
to each judgment.
- **Set-piece flow:** (1) Sneak the hall's length crossing tail segments
  only while a judgment holds them still. (2) Minos notices Dante — rears,
  blocking the exit stair (as in Canto V he challenges Dante's entry). (3)
  **Virgil confronts him** — steps onto the lectern queue plate and holds
  up the ticket; the poem's rebuke ("it is willed where what is willed
  must be") staged as Minos frozen mid-coil, quivering with rage. (4)
  While Virgil holds him, the tail is locked stiff as a bridge: the player
  **climbs and crosses the tail itself** over the open judgment floor to
  the exit stair. Virgil breaks off and follows; the tail crashes down
  behind them.
- **☠ Deaths:** (1) Crossing a segment mid-sweep → swept into a floor
  hatch. *Lesson: judgments are the metronome.* (2) Too slow in phase 4 →
  Minos shakes the tail-bridge → fall. *Lesson: Virgil buys time; he does
  not stop time.*
- **Checkpoint:** hall entry, phase 3 start.

### L8 — The Down Escalator
A vast, ancient wooden escalator descending into a rose-and-smoke haze,
wind already tugging at Dante's coat from below — Circle 2's storm,
announced before it is seen. Virgil steps on first. Dante follows. The
ribbon plaque of the next circle rises to meet the camera: **LUST**.
- **☠ Deaths:** none. Breather, foreshadow, title card.
- **Checkpoint:** level complete.

## Slice acceptance criteria

- A new player who never reads instructions completes the Prologue in
  ≤ 20 minutes with ≥ 5 deaths, each attributable to a listed lesson.
- Playtest question after L3 ("what is this place?") answered correctly —
  without any text having appeared.
- The L5 gate rhythm teaches the Signal timing well enough that ≥ 70% of
  testers clear Minos phase 4 within three attempts.
- Total slice: 17 screens, 2 set-pieces, 3 Virgil beats, zero words.
