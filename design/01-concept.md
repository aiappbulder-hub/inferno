# INFERNO — One-Page Concept

## Logline

Midway through his life, a modern man boards a train home and never surfaces:
the subway keeps descending. Guided by a station attendant from another era,
he must walk down through all nine circles of Dante's Hell — re-staged as the
transit system under the city — because the only way out is through the
bottom.

## What it is

A 2D cinematic platformer in the tradition of *Another World* (1991): no HUD,
no dialogue, one continuous camera, a fixed verb set, and fast, instructive
deaths. The story is Dante's *Inferno*, adapted canto by canto — same
guardians, same encounters, same ending — with every scene translated into
modern subway terms. Charon is a graveyard-shift conductor; Minos is a ticket
inspector whose tail is the queue barrier; Lucifer is fused into the frozen
machine heart of the system.

## Pillars

1. **No HUD, no text, no dialogue.** Diegetic transit signage only; one
   ribbon plaque names each circle.
2. **One camera, one art style.** Gameplay and cutscenes are continuous
   within a level.
3. **Death is the teacher.** Frequent, fast, fair; instant restart at a
   checkpoint per screen or set-piece.
4. **A fixed action set, recontextualized.** Walk/run, jump, crouch/crawl,
   grab/climb, use/push/pull, follow/signal Virgil. No inventory, no
   upgrades, no combat — Dante never fights.
5. **By the book.** If a scene isn't in the poem, it isn't in the game. The
   modern staging changes how it looks, never what happens.

## Player experience goals

- The dread of reading an unfamiliar screen and the satisfaction of solving
  it on the third death.
- A wordless companionship with Virgil that the player feels through
  choreography, not cutscene monologue.
- Recognition: players who know the poem see every canto land; players who
  don't experience a coherent, eerie descent through a subway that should not
  exist.

## Shape and scope

- **Length:** ~3–5 hours. Prologue + nine circle levels, 15–25 minutes each.
- **View:** side-on, screen-by-screen with cinematic pans; single resolution
  target (640×360 internal, integer-scaled).
- **Art:** chunky pixel art, ~40 px characters, flat color fields, one
  dominant color per circle.
- **Audio:** diegetic transit sound (rails, ventilation, announcements in no
  language); music only at circle title cards.
- **Team estimate:** 4–6 people (design/code, 2× pixel art & animation,
  audio, plus part-time producer/QA), 18–24 months.
- **Platforms:** PC first (Steam), then Switch-class consoles. Controller
  primary, keyboard supported. No procedural content, no live features.

## Rating & tone

T / PEGI-16. Stylized, satirical, melancholy; the horror is bureaucratic and
architectural, not gory. The damned are ordinary modern people suffering the
poem's exact punishments in transit spaces.
