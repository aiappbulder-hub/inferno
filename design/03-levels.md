# INFERNO — Nine-Circle Progression

The completed level table required by the brief: each circle's canonical
guardian in modern dress, its metro stratum, its mechanical twist on the
fixed verbs, its **famous soul** staged as a set-piece (every one traceable
to a canto), and its place in the pacing and difficulty curve.

Difficulty is rated 1–5 on two axes: **Execution** (timing/precision demands)
and **Reading** (puzzle opacity). The curve alternates peaks so no two
consecutive circles peak on the same axis.

## Pacing shape

```
Exec  1  2  4  2  3  3  3  5  4  4      (peaks: Lust, Violence)
Read  1  2  2  3  3  4  4  3  5  3      (peaks: Heresy→Wrath ramp, Fraud)
       P  1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8  9
```

- **Act 1 (P–2):** teach, then first execution spike. Ends on the Francesca
  faint — the poem's own act break.
- **Act 2 (3–7):** widen the verb recontextualization; Virgil's one visible
  failure (Dis) is the emotional midpoint; Geryon's descent is the curtain.
- **Act 3 (8–9):** the game lies to the player (Fraud), then strips
  everything to traction, wind, and one climb (Treachery). The gravity flip
  is the final new sensation, held for the last ten minutes only.

## Recurring rules across all levels

- **Ghost trains** run on every stratum, always ignorable, never boardable —
  until players notice, deep in Fraud, that the trains have stopped, and in
  Treachery that the tunnels are solid ice. The system dies as you descend.
- **Virgil's ticket** is shown at every threshold; each guardian refuses
  differently, yields differently. Ten variations on one silent gag that is
  also the poem's actual plot device.
- **Set-piece restraint rule:** each famous soul gets exactly one screen and
  no reprise. The poem is an anthology; the game honors the form.
